The family office readiness assessment

A fixed-fee engagement that answers one question: should your family have a family office? It starts with one balance sheet of everything the family owns, because no one can make that decision well without it.

What the family receives

A consolidated balance sheet

Every account, entity, trust, and private holding in one view, organized by asset class, owner, custodian, liquidity, and cost. It reports what the family holds today and becomes the foundation for every decision that follows.

The all-in cost, in one number

Advisory fees, fund management fees, carried interest, trustee fees, and the costs buried inside products, added up to one annual dollar figure and one percentage. Few families have ever seen it.

A liquidity calendar

Capital calls on unfunded commitments, lockups and redemption windows, loan maturities, estimated taxes, and expected distributions on a single timeline, so the family sees a cash crunch coming before it arrives.

A continuity test

One structured conversation about who signs, who succeeds, where the originals are, and who else knows. It answers a plain question: if the person who knows where everything is were gone tomorrow, who could step in? Every uncertain answer goes on the gap list.

A governance review

Who makes investment decisions, whether a written investment policy exists, how managers are hired and monitored, and how reporting reaches the family, covered in the same conversation.

A readiness verdict

A direct answer on whether a single family office makes sense, what it would cost to run each year, and how it compares with the realistic alternatives, such as a multi-family office or better reporting from the family's current advisors.

How it works

  1. Introductory conversation

    We discuss the family, its holdings, and its advisors, and agree on the scope and the fee.

  2. Statements

    The family provides recent account and fund statements, or authorizes me to request them from custodians and advisors directly. No legal documents are required.

  3. Consolidation and one conversation

    I build the balance sheet, the cost summary, and the liquidity calendar, and I spend about 90 minutes with the family on governance and continuity.

  4. Report and presentation

    I present the findings to the family in person and leave the written report and the balance sheet with them.

Fee

One fixed fee

I quote the fee after the first conversation, it is agreed before work begins, and it does not change with the size of the family's assets. I do not manage assets, sell investment products, or accept commissions.

If the answer is yes

I can help the family design the office, hire its staff, and select its service providers, and I can serve as its Chief Investment Officer.

Because that role is a possible outcome of the assessment, I disclose it at the first meeting so the family can weigh it.
