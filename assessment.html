The family office readiness assessment

A fixed-fee engagement that answers one question: should your family have a family office? It starts with one balance sheet of everything the family owns, because no one can make that decision well without it.

What the family receives

A consolidated balance sheet

Every holding appears once, organized by asset class, with the owner and custodian of each and every liability netted against it. It shows the family's net worth as of a stated date and marks which values are estimates.

The all-in cost

Advisory fees, fund expenses, private fund management fees, real estate management, and trustee fees, each shown with what it is charged on. Recurring costs are separated from carried interest, which varies from year to year.

A liquidity calendar

Capital calls on unfunded commitments, loan maturities, estimated taxes, and expected distributions on one timeline, with the family's minimum cash reserve marked, so a funding gap shows up months before it arrives.

A continuity test

One structured conversation about who signs, who succeeds, where the originals are, and who else knows. Each answer is recorded as the family reports it, and every uncertain answer goes on the gap list.

A governance review

Who makes investment decisions, whether a written investment policy exists, how managers are hired and monitored, and how reporting reaches the family, covered in the same conversation.

A readiness verdict

A recommendation on what structure fits: a dedicated family office, a lean or outsourced model, or better arrangements with the family's current advisors. It compares the annual cost of each and lists the first actions to take.

Inside the report

Five pages from an illustrative report. The family has holdings across two custodians, six banks, two trusts, fourteen private funds, three real estate LLCs, and an operating company. Values are as of September 30, 2026.

Readiness verdict

Recommendation: a lean family office, not a fully staffed one.

Why

  • Fourteen private funds and a dynasty trust create recurring work that none of the family's current advisors coordinates.
  • The $148.9M held outside the operating company does not yet justify a full staff.
  • A sale of the operating company would change the answer, so the verdict should be revisited at that point.

First actions

  1. Arrange refinancing or another source for the $6.5M real estate loan due July 2027. Owner: the family and its lender.
  2. Add backup signers and documented backup access for the entities and bank accounts. Owner: the estate attorney, within 60 days.
  3. Engage a consolidated reporting provider. Owner: the family, within 90 days.

Before implementation, the family should agree on who leads the office and what it is responsible for.

Who it is for

Families whose wealth has outgrown the way it is managed. That usually means some combination of:

  • several wealth managers or custodians
  • private funds or direct investments
  • trusts and family entities
  • a business that has been sold or may be
  • a generation preparing to take over

Timing and scope

The report is delivered about six weeks after the statements arrive. The engagement includes the introductory conversation, one 90-minute conversation on governance and continuity, an in-person presentation, and one round of revisions.

Missing statements delay the work but do not stop it. The report notes anything that could not be confirmed.

How it works

  1. Introductory conversation

    About 45 minutes on the family, its holdings, and its advisors. Nothing needs to be prepared. We agree on the scope and the fee.

  2. Statements

    The family provides recent account and fund statements through a secure file share I set up, or authorizes me to request them from custodians and advisors directly.

  3. Consolidation and one conversation

    I build the balance sheet, the cost summary, and the liquidity calendar, and I spend about 90 minutes with the family on governance and continuity.

  4. Report and presentation

    I present the findings and the verdict to the family in person and leave the written report and the balance sheet with them.

Please do not send statements, account numbers, or passwords by email. I set up a secure file share for each family, and documents are deleted at the end of the engagement unless the family asks me to keep them.

Fee

One fixed fee

The fee reflects the scope: the number of accounts, entities, and funds, and the meetings involved. It is quoted after the first conversation, agreed before work begins, and is not a percentage of assets. I do not manage assets or sell investment products.

If the answer is yes

I can help the family design the office, hire its staff, and select its service providers. I can also run the office, serve as its Chief Investment Officer, or both. Because that is a possible outcome, the assessment carries four safeguards:

  • The fee is the same whatever the verdict.
  • The family owns the report and can use anyone to carry it out.
  • Any role with the family's office is a separate decision, made by the family under a separate agreement.
  • I accept no referral fees or commissions from any provider I recommend.
