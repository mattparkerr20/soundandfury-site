The Family Office Readiness Assessment

A fixed-fee engagement that answers one question: should your family have a family office? It starts with one balance sheet of everything the family owns, because no one can make that decision well without it.

What the family receives

A consolidated balance sheet

Every account, trust, entity, and private holding organized by asset class, with the owner of each, assets and liabilities shown separately, and the family's net worth as of a stated date.

The all-in cost

Advisory fees, fund expenses, private fund management fees, real estate management, trustee fees, and accounting, each shown with what it is charged on. Recurring costs are separated from carried interest, which varies from year to year.

A liquidity calendar

Capital calls on unfunded commitments, loan maturities, estimated taxes, fees, and expected distributions on one timeline, measured against the family's minimum cash reserve, so a funding need shows up months before it arrives.

A continuity test

One structured conversation about who signs, who succeeds, where the originals are, and who else knows. Every uncertain answer becomes an open question for the family and its attorney.

A governance review

Who makes investment decisions, whether a written investment policy exists, how managers are hired and monitored, and how reporting reaches the family, covered in the same conversation.

A readiness verdict

A recommendation on what structure fits: a dedicated family office, a lean or outsourced model, or better arrangements with the family's current advisors. It compares what each would cost the family in total and lists the first actions to take.

Inside the report

Five pages from a report for an illustrative family with two custodians, six banks, two trusts, fourteen private funds, three real estate LLCs, and an operating company, as of September 30, 2026.

Readiness verdict

Recommendation: a lean family office with one investment lead and outsourced accounting and reporting.

Why

  • The family needs one person accountable for its investments: fourteen private funds, $15.0M of unfunded commitments, a credit line, and a loan maturing in 2027. No current advisor owns that work.
  • The accounting, reporting, and administrative workload is routine and can be outsourced, so a full staff would add cost without adding judgment.
  • Consolidated reporting alone would organize the information but leave no one responsible for acting on it.
  • A sale of the operating company could change the answer, depending on the proceeds and what the family wants to do with them.

First actions

  1. Decide by March 31, 2027 how to fund the $6.5M loan due in July: refinance it or draw on the credit line. Owner: the family's lead decision-maker, with the lender and the family's investment advisor.
  2. Add backup signers and documented backup access for the entities and bank accounts. Owner: the family, with its estate attorney.
  3. Recruit the investment lead and select an outsourced accounting and reporting provider. Owner: the family, within six months.

Office cost estimates draw on Campden Wealth's 2025 Family Office Operational Excellence Report ($1.0M to $1.5M a year for offices under $250M), Simon Quick Advisors ($1M to $2M for offices with up to six employees), the Heidrick & Struggles 2025 Family Offices Compensation Survey, published reporting platform pricing, and industry surveys of multi-family office fees.

Who it is for

Families whose holdings have outgrown their current reporting. That usually means some combination of:

  • several wealth managers or custodians
  • private funds or direct investments
  • trusts and family entities
  • a business that has been sold or may be
  • a generation preparing to take over

Families that already have an office use the same work as an independent review of how it is running: what it costs, what it holds, and what happens if a key person leaves.

Timing and scope

The report is delivered about six weeks after the statements arrive. The engagement includes the introductory conversation, one 90-minute conversation on governance and continuity, an in-person presentation, and one round of revisions.

Missing statements delay the work but do not stop it. The report notes anything that could not be confirmed.

How it works

  1. Introductory conversation

    About 45 minutes on the family, its holdings, and its advisors. Nothing needs to be prepared. We agree on the scope and the fee.

  2. Statements

    The family provides recent account and fund statements through a secure upload link I provide, or authorizes me to request them from custodians and advisors directly.

  3. Consolidation and one conversation

    I build the balance sheet, the cost summary, and the liquidity calendar, and I spend about 90 minutes with the family on governance and continuity.

  4. Report and presentation

    I present the findings and the verdict to the family in person and leave the written report and the balance sheet with them.

A confidentiality agreement is signed before any statements are shared. Please do not send statements, account numbers, or passwords by email. At the end of the engagement I return or delete the family's documents unless the family asks me to keep them.

For attorneys and accountants

I send the open questions to the family's attorney before the family sees the report, and counsel is welcome at the presentation. I do not interpret legal documents or give legal or tax advice. When the family wants privilege considered, I can be engaged through its counsel.

References from prior families, and from the attorneys and accountants who worked alongside me, are available on request.

Fee

One fixed fee

The fee is typically $25,000 to $60,000. It reflects the scope: the number of accounts, entities, and funds, and the meetings involved. It is quoted after the first conversation, agreed before work begins, and is not a percentage of assets. It is the same whatever the verdict, the family owns the report, and I accept no referral fees or commissions from any provider I recommend, and I pay none for referrals.
